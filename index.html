On paper, I was untouchable.

Forty straight quarters of hitting targets. Global clients. Awards. The big exit everyone in our world quietly fantasizes about.

$50 million. My company. My name on the virtual door.

From the outside, it looked like the happily-ever-after. 10 years of succesful leadership, consistent performance, Then one afternoon, I I was asked to leave the very company I built.

Not a dramatic screaming match. No flipped tables. Just a calm, surgical conversation.

“We’re going in a different direction. We’re grateful for everything you’ve done.”

I nodded like a professional. I even thanked them.

I walked out with a nice package, a clean story, and a single brutal truth echoing in my head:

I didn’t know who I was without this.

"Imposter syndrome would be an understatement"

That shock didn’t just sting my ego, it cracked my entire identity.

For the first time in years, I wasn’t “Keith, the founder who sold for $50M.” I was just a guy sitting in a quiet house in the middle of the day, staring at a calendar with no meetings on it.

And when the noise stopped, I started seeing the industry I’d helped build with brutal clarity.

An industry obsessed with vanity:

  • Titles that sound bigger than the actual impact
  • Follower counts treated like net worth
  • Engagement metrics used as a substitute for real connection

I’d spent years inside a machine that rewarded who was seen the most, not who was actually telling the truth.

We celebrated “reach” more than we celebrated integrity. We chased impressions while people behind the dashboards were quietly burning out, breaking down, or numbing themselves just to keep performing.

Losing my job forced a hard question:

If all the numbers disappeared, no title, no audience, no “congratulations” posts, who was I actually in relationship with? Who would still pick up the phone? Who really knew me?

That’s where everything flipped for me.

I stopped believing influence was measured in followers, impressions, or likes. Influence, to me now, is measured in conversations:

  • Who trusts you enough to talk to you live
  • Who feels safe telling you the unpolished version of their story
  • Who shows up again tomorrow, not because the algorithm pushed you, but because the connection is real

That realization is what pulled me out of the old game.
